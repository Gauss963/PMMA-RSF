\begin{abstract}
This note records the 32 normal-displacement dip cases \run{0208} through
\run{0239}.  The prescribed displacement on the moving block's normal-loading
face varies linearly along the fault while its endpoint mean remains equal to
the calibrated uniform displacement.  The first 16 cases cover endpoint
deviations from $-10\%$ to $+10\%$; the second 16 add both orientations between
$10\%$ and $20\%$ without repeating a case.  All other geometry, material,
rate-and-state friction, shear loading, time integration, and output settings
are held fixed.  Of the 32 simulations, 29 completed and three stopped because
of GPU out-of-memory errors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definition of the sweep}

Let $y=0$ be the loading end, $y=L=500\ \mathrm{mm}$ the leading end, and
$u_0=0.6916209867\ \mathrm{mm}$ the uniform normal-displacement calibration for
approximately $16\ \mathrm{MPa}$.  Each case prescribes
\begin{equation}
  u_n(y)=u_0\left[m_{\rm load}
  +(m_{\rm lead}-m_{\rm load})\frac{y}{L}\right].
  \label{eq:normal-dip}
\end{equation}
Every pair satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \frac{m_{\rm load}+m_{\rm lead}}{2}=1,
\end{equation}
so the line-average prescribed displacement is $u_0$.  The sweep therefore
changes the normal-load gradient and its orientation without intentionally
changing the mean compression.  This statement concerns the prescribed
boundary displacement; elastic edge effects mean that the resulting fault
normal stress need not be exactly linear or have an exactly uniform mean.

The two deterministic generators are
\begin{quote}\small
\file{scripts/generate_ts0163_normal_dip_sweep_cases.py},\\
\file{scripts/generate_ts0163_normal_dip20_outer_sweep_cases.py}.
\end{quote}
They use \file{cases/rsf_0163_leading_interp_04.toml} as the common source
case and explicitly remove its leading-edge chamfer.

The loading face is locked in shear during the normal phase.  Tangential
contact remains active, and no normal-relaxation or quasi-static damping stage
is used.  Consequently, changes across the sweep can be attributed to the
normal-displacement profile, subject to numerical failure or stochastic
hardware effects.

\section{Data and analysis products}

Each run's standard static figures are stored in \file{runs/TSxxxx/Plot} and
tabular analysis products in \file{runs/TSxxxx/stats}.  The normal-loading
diagnostic added for this campaign is
\begin{quote}\small
\file{Plot/normal_phase_end_normal_stress_profiles.pdf},\\
\file{stats/normal_phase_end_normal_stress_profiles.csv},\\
\file{stats/normal_phase_end_normal_stress_profiles.json}.
\end{quote}
It samples compressive-positive $-\sigma_{xx}$ at the last normal-phase bulk
frame, both in the first moving-block element layer adjacent to the loading
boundary and 5 mm from the fault on the moving side.  For the $0.5\ \mathrm{mm}$
mesh, the actual element-center coordinates are $x=0.25\ \mathrm{mm}$ and the
average of $x=194.75$ and $195.25\ \mathrm{mm}$, respectively.  The three OOM
runs retained their normal-phase frames, so this normal-loading diagnostic is
valid even though their shear-dependent figures are incomplete.

At the time of the status audit, the F1 checkout retained status and plot
directories for all 32 cases, but the bulk HDF5 files for \run{0208}--\run{0223}
were no longer present under their standard run paths.  Reconstructing any new
bulk-field diagnostic for those 16 cases requires restoring their original
\file{data/simulation.h5} files.
